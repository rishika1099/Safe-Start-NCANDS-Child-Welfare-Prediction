# Results — Simulated Data Pipeline

> Generated by [`results/run_analysis.py`](run_analysis.py). All inputs are synthetic; all numbers below describe the simulated dataset only.

## Setup

- Inputs: [`data/acs_scr_reports.csv`](../data/acs_scr_reports.csv) and [`data/acs_features.csv`](../data/acs_features.csv).
- 1,000 simulated SCR reports spanning **2021-01-01 → 2024-12-31**.
- 498 unique families (mean ~2 reports per family) — drives group-aware splitting.
- Base rate of the modeling target `needs_investigative_consultation` = **25.0%**.
- Models trained: regularized logistic regression (L2, `class_weight="balanced"`), random forest, and gradient boosting.
- Cross-validation: `GroupKFold(n_splits=5)` keyed on `family_id` to prevent family-level leakage.
- Note: the NCANDS plan uses XGBoost; this script uses sklearn's GradientBoosting because XGBoost requires `libomp` which was not available in the sandbox. The two are interchangeable for the methodology shown here.

## 1. Descriptive

- Volume by borough is concentrated in Bronx (n=299) and Brooklyn (n=274), with Staten Island the smallest (n=67) — see [tables/02_volume_by_borough.csv](tables/02_volume_by_borough.csv).
- Substantiation rate (the *outcome* in the SCR file, distinct from the modeling target) is **28.2%** overall.
- Highest substantiation rates by reporter type are **Social Service Provider (41.7%)** and **Neighbor/Community (38.5%)**; lowest is **Daycare/Childcare (22.6%)**. See [tables/03_reporter_substantiation.csv](tables/03_reporter_substantiation.csv).
- Monthly volume trend is stable across 2021–2024 (no obvious seasonality or shock in the simulated draws): [figures/fig01_volume_trend.png](figures/fig01_volume_trend.png).

## 3. Cross-validated performance (group-aware 5-fold CV)

| Model | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Brier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| logistic_l2 | **0.616** | **0.381** | 0.239 |
| random_forest | 0.612 | 0.345 | 0.196 |
| gradient_boosting | 0.591 | 0.330 | 0.200 |

The regularized logistic regression is the best by PR-AUC and ROC-AUC on this dataset. Random forest has the best Brier score (best calibration) but worse discrimination — typical for a tree ensemble under class imbalance without calibration. Full per-fold metrics in [tables/04_cv_fold_metrics.csv](tables/04_cv_fold_metrics.csv); summary in [tables/05_cv_summary.csv](tables/05_cv_summary.csv); ROC/PR curves in [figures/fig04_roc_pr.png](figures/fig04_roc_pr.png).

**Read.** With a 25% prevalence, a PR-AUC of 0.38 means the model achieves a real but modest lift over the base rate; this is the expected behavior on a small synthetic dataset where most relationships are weak. The methodology — group CV, missing indicators, imbalance-aware loss — is the point, not the magnitude of the AUC.

## 4. Thresholding

Two thresholds are reported because the F2-maximizing operating point degenerates here:

| Threshold rule | Cutoff | Flag rate | Precision | Recall | F2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| F2-max (unconstrained) | 0.050 | 100.0% | 0.250 | 1.000 | 0.625 |
| **Operational (top-25%)** | **0.585** | **25.0%** | **0.368** | **0.368** | **0.368** |

Because the signal is weak, an unconstrained F2 optimizer prefers "flag everyone" (recall=1, precision=base rate). Under an operational caseload constraint — flag the top-25% of cases, matching prevalence — precision and recall both rise to **0.37**, a real 1.5× lift over the 0.25 prior. The full sweep is in [tables/06_threshold_sweep.csv](tables/06_threshold_sweep.csv) and visualized in [figures/fig03_threshold_sweep.png](figures/fig03_threshold_sweep.png). The operational point is the threshold used for all downstream fairness analysis.

## 5. Fairness audit at the operational threshold

### By borough — [tables/07_fairness_borough.csv](tables/07_fairness_borough.csv) · [figures/fig05_fairness_borough.png](figures/fig05_fairness_borough.png)

| Borough | n | Positive | Flag rate | Precision | Recall | FPR | ROC-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bronx | 299 | 0.224 | 0.197 | 0.322 | 0.284 | 0.172 | 0.577 |
| Brooklyn | 274 | 0.296 | **0.361** | 0.394 | **0.481** | **0.311** | 0.610 |
| Manhattan | 180 | 0.300 | 0.328 | 0.407 | 0.444 | 0.278 | **0.643** |
| Queens | 180 | 0.189 | **0.106** | 0.368 | **0.206** | **0.082** | 0.577 |
| Staten Island | 67 | 0.209 | 0.209 | 0.214 | 0.214 | 0.208 | 0.516 |

**Read.** Brooklyn is **over-flagged** at this threshold (FPR 0.31, ~4× Queens' 0.08) and **Queens is under-flagged** (recall 0.21 vs Brooklyn's 0.48). The model also discriminates much better in Manhattan (AUC 0.64) than in Staten Island (AUC 0.52). In a real deployment this is the kind of finding that would need to be discussed with operational stakeholders before any threshold is shipped — either by setting borough-specific thresholds, applying post-hoc calibration per group, or revisiting feature selection. The very small Staten Island sample (n=67) also signals that any per-borough conclusion there is statistically fragile.

## 6. SHAP explainability

Global importance (mean |SHAP|) on the gradient-boosting model, top features — full list in [tables/09_shap_importance.csv](tables/09_shap_importance.csv); chart in [figures/fig06_shap_top15.png](figures/fig06_shap_top15.png):

1. `reporter_accuracy_score` (0.31)
2. `child_age_under_5` (0.22)
3. `caseworker_caseload` (0.21)
4. `prior_reports_12mo` (0.18)
5. `prior_substantiated_flag` (0.14)
6. `days_since_last_report` (0.13)
7. `days_to_first_contact` (0.12)
8. `reporter_type=Law Enforcement` (0.10)
9. `allegation_type=Neglect - Educational` (0.10)
10. `substance_use_flag` (0.09)

**Read.** The two strongest signals are **operational** features (reporter accuracy, caseworker caseload), not child-level risk features. This is a known and important pattern in real child welfare modeling: administrative features that describe the *system handling the case* can dominate the score, which means the model is partly learning who reports cases and who handles them, not just whether the child is at risk. Prior-involvement features (`prior_reports_12mo`, `prior_substantiated_flag`, `days_since_last_report`) rank next, which is consistent with the simulation design.

## Headline takeaways

1. **Methodology transfers; magnitude doesn't.** PR-AUC of 0.38 on 1k synthetic rows is exactly the regime where the *shape* of the pipeline matters more than the *number* — group-aware CV, missing indicators, threshold-under-constraint, disaggregated fairness, and SHAP all work as expected and produce interpretable artifacts.
2. **F2 alone is the wrong objective when signal is weak.** It collapses to "flag everyone." The realistic constraint is operational capacity; the top-K% threshold is what should be reported.
3. **Geography is the largest fairness disparity in the simulated data.** Brooklyn FPR is ~4× Queens'. In a real deployment this would gate the threshold choice.
4. **Operational features dominate SHAP.** Reporter and caseworker features outweigh child-level risk features. Worth flagging before any model is interpreted as "predicting child risk" — it's partly predicting *who reports and who handles cases*.
